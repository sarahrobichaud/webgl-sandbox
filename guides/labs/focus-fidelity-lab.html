Focus & Fidelity Lab

Your empire has many airports. Only one should feel like Airport CEO on a grid. The others stay on the map as simplified businesses you can still deal with. This lab is about that attention model — the core architectural idea of the product.

idea: one full grid at a time same business: two representations no code: mental model only
1 · The product promise 2 · Two fidelities, one identity 3 · Watch focus switch (lab) 4 · What must exist when? 5 · Zoom-out policy (decide later) 6 · What to protect in the design

01 The product promise

In plain language:

  • World map → found businesses (airports) in different places.
  • Focus one → enter a blank (then built-up) site world — full simulation.
  • Zoom out → empire view again; start or visit other businesses.
  • Background sites: not a second full grid ticking secretly — simplified outputs + interactions (contracts, policies).
This is both a fun design and a performance design. A web game cannot honestly run N detailed airport sims at once. Architecture should match the promise.

02 Two fidelities, one identity

Same business in the empire. Different runtime representation depending on focus.

FULL · focused site

Detailed grid world. Buildings, queues, local ops, camera, placement.

  • needs engine grid / world tools
  • expensive to tick
  • what the player “enters”

ABSTRACT · background business

Rolled-up state. Daily/periodic outputs. Accepts empire commands.

  • formulas, events, summaries
  • cheap to tick (or resolve on demand)
  • map pin + deal UI, not a hidden twin grid
Identity lives in the game (empire). Fidelity is how that identity is currently hosted — full world mounted, or abstract projection only.

03 Watch focus switch

Three airports on one empire clock. Only the focused one burns “detail work.” Background sites emit simplified income. Switch focus and watch who is expensive.

Empire clock · focus = full fidelity
Map view ≠ all airports go abstract automatically — that’s a policy choice (section 5). This lab’s default: map view still keeps the last focused site as the “full” candidate, but you can treat map mode as “no full grid mounted” in a real design.

Guess whether each thing needs the full site world, only abstract business state, or the empire layer.

05 Zoom-out policy (decide later)

When the player leaves a site for the world map, you eventually pick one story:

PolicyBehaviorTradeoff
A · Demote on leave Leaving site collapses full world → abstract (maybe snapshot first). Honest web budget; focus switch is a real event.
B · Keep warm Last site keeps full sim in memory while on the map; others abstract. Snappier re-enter; still only one full world.
C · Full always (trap) Every airport keeps a detailed world. Breaks the product promise and the browser.
You don’t need to pick A vs B today. You do need to reject C as a design default, and name focus change as a first-class game event later.

06 What to protect in the design

Invariants worth keeping

Empire owns identities — businesses, map pins, shared money, contracts.

At most one full site world mounted for play (engine specialty).

Abstract ≠ fake full world — formulas and deals, not a silent second grid.

Views read; sims write — map and site scenes project state; they don’t own empire cash.
